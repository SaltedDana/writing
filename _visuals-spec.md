# Visuals Spec — one additional visual per article

Last updated: 2026-08-20 00:44

Design system: June (`~/Dana_OS/projects/june-design-system/june.css`). Flat 90-degree color blocks only. No rounded corners, no gradients, no shadows. Six tokens: orange `#FC7142`, lavender `#CDACFE`, sage `#D1DDD4`, sky `#F5F9FC`, blue `#3292FF`, slate `#7BA3AD`, plus ink `#4A4A4D` and paper `#FFFFFF`. Shapes limited to rect, square, diamond (square rotated 45°), bar. Type: Rubik Mono One for labels/headlines, Manrope for captions (English/LTR override), JetBrains Mono for numbers and stat values.

Scope: this file is the spec only, for review before any building. Nothing is rendered. The existing `cover.jpg` in every folder stays untouched; every plan below is an additional, second visual placed mid-article. Fifteen articles get a hand-coded SVG diagram, three get an image-generation prompt, one gets no additional visual (explained inline).

## taste-isnt-the-moat-youre-guarding

**Classification:** DIAGRAM

**Core argument:** Taste is a verdict that converges as every model release raises the baseline; sense, the judgment of whether something is worth making at all, doesn't converge — that's the part worth defending.

**Diagram plan:** Two-column split block, viewBox roughly 700x320.
- Left rect, `bg-orange`, label "TASTE" in `--font-display`, sublabel "Verdict. Converges." in `--font-sub`. Inside it, four small mono-labeled bars of decreasing height (left to right) to show the gap shrinking with every release.
- Right rect, `bg-blue`, label "SENSE" sublabel "Aim. Doesn't converge." Inside it, four bars of equal height, same color, to show no convergence.
- A single `--june-ink` horizontal bar (`.j-bar`) spans the bottom as a strict divider.
- Caption beneath in `--font-mono`: "Automate the file. Defend the stance."

**Placement:** Directly after the paragraph "Taste is a verdict... Sense is an aim..." (the line defining the two terms), before the "Watch the gap" bulleted examples — so the split lands before the reader hits the worked examples.

## my-hack-for-consistency-that-isnt

**Classification:** DIAGRAM

**Core argument:** Discipline runs on a depleting stress-push (norepinephrine); curiosity runs on a sustaining reward-pull (dopamine) — reframing obligation as investigation is what makes consistency last.

**Diagram plan:** Two stacked horizontal bars, viewBox roughly 700x260.
- Top `.j-bar`, `bg-orange`, label "DISCIPLINE — PUSH", mono sublabel "norepinephrine". A small square marker positioned left, shown shrinking toward the right end of the bar to signal depletion.
- Bottom `.j-bar`, `bg-lavender`, label "CURIOSITY — PULL", mono sublabel "dopamine". A small square marker of constant size, evenly spaced across the bar, to signal it doesn't deplete.
- Two short arrows above each bar: one pointing away from a square (pushed), one pointing toward a square (pulled) — reinforcing push vs. pull without any curved motion lines.

**Placement:** Right after the "The Dopamine Loop" heading, before the "Why not discipline?" paragraph — the diagram should set up the contrast before the text unpacks it.

```
I want a single visual for this piece, in the visual language of my brand, Salted Mind.

Texture: a subtle dotted grid or graph-paper background is allowed. No emojis, no 3D, no glow, no corporate stock-diagram look.

Metaphor to use: a kitchen. One plate holding both a dessert and a spoonful of medicine, or two small side-by-side servings, dessert and cough syrup, the exact object described in the essay. Use this metaphor and no other — don't blend it with anything else.

Composition idea to consider (adapt freely): a simple kitchen counter sketch, one small dessert dish and one spoon of syrup sitting side by side, same size, same plate style, so the visual reads as "same act, different frame" rather than as two different activities.

Deliver in this order:
1. A one-paragraph description of the composition you chose and why it fits.
2. The image (or an SVG/Mermaid sketch first, if image generation is off).
3. A short list of what you deliberately left out and why.

Don't ask clarifying questions before the first pass. Show me something, then I'll react.
```

**Placement:** Right after "I wanted my brain to log swimming as something enjoyable... you rush through because it's 'good for you.'" — the visual should land on the exact sentence it illustrates.
